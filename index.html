PART III • ARTICLES 12–35
Fundamental Rights (The Golden Triangle)

Article 14 (Equality before Law & Non-arbitrariness), Article 19(1)(a)/(g) (Freedom of Speech, Assembly & Profession), and Article 21 (Protection of Life, Personal Liberty & Privacy under Maneka Gandhi & Puttaswamy).

PART III • ARTICLES 32 & 226
Constitutional Writs & Judicial Review

The heart and soul of the Constitution. Direct enforcement of Fundamental Rights before the Supreme Court (Art. 32) and High Courts (Art. 226) via Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, and Quo Warranto.

ARTICLE 368 • CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS
Basic Structure Doctrine & Amending Power

Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368 is subject to the inviolable Basic Structure Doctrine established in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973 13-Judge Bench).

PART IV & IV-A • ARTICLES 36–51A
Directive Principles & Fundamental Duties

Governance obligations of the State (welfare, living wage, equal pay, free legal aid under Art. 39A) and the 11 Fundamental Duties of citizens under Article 51A.

⚖️ Supreme Court Bench Comparison: Side-by-Side Analysis
Comparing constitutional ratios and statutory interpretations across landmark Indian benches.
CASE A • PRIMARY PRECEDENT
Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India
Supreme Court • 1978 (7-Judge Bench)
Key Facts: Impounding of petitioner's passport without providing prior hearing or reasons under Section 10(3)(c) of Passports Act 1967.
Constitutional Issues: Whether Article 21 procedural law can be arbitrary, and relationship between Articles 14, 19, and 21.
Judgment Ratio: Any statutory procedure depriving personal liberty under Art. 21 must be "just, fair, and reasonable" and satisfy Art. 14 equality.
CASE B • COMPARATIVE PRECEDENT
Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India
Supreme Court • 2017 (9-Judge Bench)
Key Facts: Constitutional challenge to Aadhaar biometric database and state surveillance on citizen privacy grounds.
Constitutional Issues: Whether Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right guaranteed under Article 21 and Part III.
Judgment Ratio: Privacy is an intrinsic Fundamental Right under Article 21. Any state restriction requires Legality, Legitimate State Aim, and Proportionality.
