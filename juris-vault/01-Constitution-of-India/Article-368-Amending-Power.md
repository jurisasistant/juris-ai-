# 📜 Article 368: Parliament's Power to Amend the Constitution
*Part XX • Amending Power & Basic Structure Limitations*
**Connected Nodes:** [[Basic-Structure-Doctrine]] • [[Kesavananda-Bharati-v-State-of-Kerala-1973]] • [[Article-32-and-226-Constitutional-Writs]]

---

## 📌 Scope of Article 368
Article 368 empowers Parliament to amend by way of addition, variation, or repeal any provision of the Constitution in accordance with the prescribed procedure (special majority in both Houses of Parliament, and state ratification for federal provisions).

---

## ⚖️ Judicial Limitation: The Basic Structure Doctrine
In **[[Kesavananda-Bharati-v-State-of-Kerala-1973]]**, a 13-Judge Constitution Bench ruled by a 7–6 majority that:
* Parliament's amending power under Article 368 is broad, but **not absolute**.
* Parliament **cannot alter, abridge, or destroy the fundamental Basic Structure** of the Constitution.
* Any constitutional amendment that damages basic features (such as judicial review, secularism, equality, or federalism) is unconstitutional and void.
