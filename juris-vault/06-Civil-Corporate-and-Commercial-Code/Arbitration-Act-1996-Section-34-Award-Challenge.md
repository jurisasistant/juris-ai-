# ⚖️ Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996 Section 34: Setting Aside Arbitral Awards
*Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 • Minimal Judicial Intervention*
**Connected Nodes:** [[Indian-Stamp-Act-Section-35-Inadmissibility]] • [[Indian-Contract-Act-Section-74-Liquidated-Damages]]

---

## 📌 Statutory Mandates (Section 34)
An arbitral award may be set aside by a civil court only on narrow statutory grounds:
1. Party incapacity or invalid arbitration agreement.
2. Improper notice of appointment of arbitrator or arbitral proceedings.
3. Award deals with a dispute not falling within the terms of submission to arbitration.
4. The composition of the arbitral tribunal or procedure was not in accordance with the agreement.
5. Award is in conflict with the **Public Policy of India** (affected by fraud/corruption, or in conflict with basic notions of morality or justice).
6. Award is vitiated by **Patent Illegality** appearing on the face of the award.

---

## 🏛️ Landmark Precedents
* **BALCO v. Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services (SC Constitution Bench 2012):** Indian courts cannot intervene in foreign-seated international arbitrations under Part I of the Act.
* **Associate Builders v. DDA (SC 2015):** Defined the boundaries of "Public Policy of India" and "Patent Illegality".
