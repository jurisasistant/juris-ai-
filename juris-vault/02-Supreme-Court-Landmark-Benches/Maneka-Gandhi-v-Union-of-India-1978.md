# 🏛️ Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
*Supreme Court of India • 7-Judge Bench • (1978) 1 SCC 248*
**Connected Nodes:** [[Article-21-Right-to-Life-and-Privacy]] • [[Article-14-Equality-Before-Law]] • [[Article-19-1-a-Freedom-of-Speech]] • [[Basic-Structure-Doctrine]]

---

## 📌 Case Summary & Constitutional Benchmark
A landmark 7-Judge Constitution Bench decision that revolutionized Indian administrative and constitutional law by reading **due process of law** into **[[Article-21-Right-to-Life-and-Privacy]]**.

---

## ⚖️ Key Facts & Constitutional Issues
* **Facts:** Petitioner Maneka Gandhi's passport was impounded by the Regional Passport Officer under Section 10(3)(c) of the Passports Act 1967 "in public interest" without providing any prior hearing or written reasons.
* **Core Issue:** Whether a statutory procedure depriving personal liberty under Article 21 can be arbitrary, and whether Articles 14, 19, and 21 operate in isolation.

---

## 🏛️ Judgment & Ratio Decidendi
1. **The Golden Triangle:** Articles 14, 19, and 21 are not mutually exclusive; a law depriving personal liberty under Article 21 must also satisfy the non-arbitrariness test of [[Article-14-Equality-Before-Law]] and freedoms under Article 19.
2. **Just, Fair, and Reasonable:** The expression "procedure established by law" under Article 21 cannot be arbitrary or unfair. It must be **just, fair, and reasonable**.
3. **Natural Justice:** Administrative orders impounding passports or depriving liberty must observe the right to be heard (*Audi Alteram Partem*).
