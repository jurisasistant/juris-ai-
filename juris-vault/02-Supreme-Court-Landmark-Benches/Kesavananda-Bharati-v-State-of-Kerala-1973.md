# 🏛️ Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
*Supreme Court of India • 13-Judge Constitution Bench • (1973) 4 SCC 225*
**Connected Nodes:** [[Basic-Structure-Doctrine]] • [[Article-368-Amending-Power]] • [[Article-32-and-226-Constitutional-Writs]]

---

## 📌 Case Summary & Constitutional Benchmark
In the largest Constitution Bench in Indian judicial history (13 Judges), the Supreme Court ruled by a 7–6 majority that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368 is subject to the **[[Basic-Structure-Doctrine]]**.

---

## ⚖️ Key Facts & Constitutional Issues
* **Petitioner:** Kesavananda Bharati, head of Edneer Mutt in Kerala, challenged state land reform legislation and the 24th, 25th, and 29th Constitutional Amendment Acts.
* **Core Issue:** Whether Parliament has an unlimited, sovereign power under Article 368 to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights.

---

## 🏛️ Judgment & Ratio Decidendi
1. Parliament has broad power to amend the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights.
2. However, **Parliament cannot alter or destroy the basic structure or essential framework of the Constitution**.
3. Reaffirmed that judicial review and Fundamental Rights form part of the inviolable Basic Structure.
