# 🏛️ Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)
*Supreme Court of India • 2-Judge Bench • (2014) 8 SCC 273*
**Connected Nodes:** [[BNSS-2023-Section-35-Arrest-Notice]] • [[Article-21-Right-to-Life-and-Privacy]] • [[IPC-Section-498A]]

---

## 📌 Case Summary & Constitutional Benchmark
Laid down binding national guidelines restricting automatic police arrest in offenses punishable with imprisonment up to **7 years** (specifically matrimonial cruelty under IPC 498A), now statutory codified under **[[BNSS-2023-Section-35-Arrest-Notice]]**.

---

## ⚖️ Key Facts & Constitutional Issues
* **Facts:** Appeal challenging mechanical police arrests under IPC Section 498A without investigating the necessity of arrest.
* **Core Issue:** Preventing arbitrary police detention and protecting personal liberty under [[Article-21-Right-to-Life-and-Privacy]].

---

## 🏛️ Judgment & Ratio Decidendi
1. Police officers must not automatically arrest when a case under Section 498A or any offense punishable up to 7 years is registered.
2. Police must issue a formal **Notice of Appearance** (old CrPC 41A, now **[[BNSS-2023-Section-35-Arrest-Notice]]**).
3. Any arrest without recording written reasons of necessity triggers contempt of court against the police officer and departmental action against the Magistrate authorizing remand.
