# 🏛️ Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010)
*Supreme Court of India • 3-Judge Bench • (2010) 7 SCC 263*
**Connected Nodes:** [[Article-20-Protection-in-Conviction]] • [[Article-21-Right-to-Life-and-Privacy]] • [[BSA-2023-Section-104-Burden-of-Proof]]

---

## 📌 Case Summary & Constitutional Benchmark
Ruled that involuntary administration of neuroscientific interrogation techniques—including **narco-analysis, polygraph (lie-detector), and Brain Electrical Oscillation Signature (BEOS) brain-mapping**—violates the right against self-incrimination under Article 20(3) and personal liberty under Article 21.

---

## ⚖️ Judgment Ratio
1. **Mental Privacy:** Personal liberty under Article 21 protects physical as well as mental privacy.
2. **Voluntary Consent Required:** Such tests can only be administered with the voluntary consent of the accused before a Judicial Magistrate.
3. **Evidentiary Admissibility:** Even with consent, statement results from narco-analysis are not directly admissible in evidence; only independently discovered physical evidence is admissible under Section 27 of Evidence Act / BSA 2023.
