# 🏛️ Basic Structure Doctrine
*Core Constitutional Theory of India*

## 📌 Principle & Definition
The **Basic Structure Doctrine** is a judicial principle established by the 13-Judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India in **[[Kesavananda-Bharati-v-State-of-Kerala-1973]]**. It holds that while Parliament has broad power to amend any part of the Constitution under Article 368, it **cannot alter, abridge, or destroy the fundamental basic structure** of the Constitution.

---

## ⚖️ Components of the Basic Structure
Indian constitutional jurisprudence recognizes several inviolable basic features:
1. **Supremacy of the Constitution**
2. **Sovereign, Democratic, and Republican nature of the Indian polity**
3. **Secular character of the Constitution**
4. **Separation of powers between the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary**
5. **Power of Judicial Review** under [[Article-32-and-226-Constitutional-Writs]] (*L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India, 1997*)
6. **The Golden Triangle of Rights:** [[Article-14-Equality-Before-Law]], [[Article-19-1-a-Freedom-of-Speech]], and [[Article-21-Right-to-Life-and-Privacy]]
7. **Rule of Law and non-arbitrariness**
